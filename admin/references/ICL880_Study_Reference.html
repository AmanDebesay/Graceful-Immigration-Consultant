▼

  • s.3(1)(e) — Grant of citizenship

  • s.5(1) — Adults (≥18)

Three Ways to Acquire Canadian Citizenship

▼
Jus Soli (Law of the Soil)
  • Birth in Canada
  • Automatic citizenship
  • Exception: Children of foreign diplomats
Jus Sanguinis (Law of the Blood)
  • Birth to Canadian citizen abroad
  • Subject to second generation cut-off
  • See Bill C-3 reforms below
Naturalization
  • Grant of citizenship (s.5)
  • Must be PR first
  • Meet all s.5(1) requirements
Special Circumstances — s.2(2)(a)
A person is deemed to be born in Canada if born on a Canadian vessel or on an aircraft registered in Canada.
🌍

Second Generation Cut-Off & Bill C-3 (2025)

▼
Previous Law (Limited Citizenship by Descent)
Child born outside Canada would be a Canadian citizen if at time of birth: (1) One parent was born in Canada OR (2) One parent became citizen by naturalization.

Example: Linda born in Canada → marries non-Canadian Jack → son Dean born in Philippines → Dean is Canadian by descent.

New Law — Second Generation Cut-Off (In Effect Since 2008)
⚠️ Second generation born abroad generally NOT entitled to citizenship by descent. Bjorkquist v. AG Canada (2023 ONSC 7152) found this unconstitutional under Charter ss.6 and 15.
Bill C-3 (June 2025 Reforms)
Citizenship for those previously denied under second generation rule Substantial connection test going forward
✅ Substantial Connection Test: Parent must demonstrate 1,095 days of physical presence in Canada prior to child's birth to pass citizenship to second generation born abroad.
📋

Grant of Citizenship — s.5(1) Requirements (Adults ≥18)

▼
  • A. Minimum 18 years of age
  • B. Permanent resident
  • C. Resident in Canada for 3 years (1,095 days) out of 5 years prior to application date
  • D. Meet income tax filing obligations for each of 3 years (if required by CRA)
  • E. Count time as temporary resident (each day = ½ day, max 365 days)
  • F. Knowledge of English OR French
  • G. Knowledge of Canada and responsibilities/privileges of citizenship
  • H. NOT under removal order or other prohibitions under the Citizenship Act
⏱️

Residency Calculation & Evidence

▼
1,095 days physically present in Canada during 5 years immediately before application
Temporary Resident / Protected Person Credit
If you were a TR or protected person during the 5 years before application: Each day counts as ½ day, with a maximum of 365 days towards physical presence calculation.
Travel History Report
CBSA now tracks exits in addition to entries (since 2015). Can request "Travel History Report" through Representative Portal — details all CBSA Entry/Exit records. Reduces uncertainty about days accumulated.

Citizenship for Adopted Children — s.5.1(1)

▼
Six Requirements for Adopted Children
  • Full adoption for child under 18 years of age
  • At least one adoptive parent is a Canadian citizen
  • Adoption must be in the best interests of the child
  • Adoption must be valid in the country where it took place
  • Must create a parent-child relationship in law and in fact
  • Cannot be a relationship of convenience for immigration/citizenship purposes
Adoption of Convenience (Red Flag)
⚠️ Exists when Canadians adopt a child of their relatives outside Canada solely to assist child's admission to Canada. Legal, emotional, financial ties with biological parents have NOT ceased and adoptive couple has NOT established genuine parent-child relationship.
Two Routes for Adopted Children
Direct Route
  • Directly apply for citizenship certificate
  • ⚠️ Limited ability to pass citizenship to own children born abroad (unless other parent born/naturalized in Canada)
Regular Route (Recommended)
  • Sponsored by adoptive parents → become PR → apply for citizenship
  • ✅ Can pass citizenship to children born abroad + apply for direct grant for adopted children

Special Cases — Exceptions to Residency

▼
Three Categories Exempt from Physical Presence Requirement
  • A. PR employed with Canadian Armed Forces, federal public administration, or public service of province/territory
  • B. Applicant outside Canada accompanying Canadian citizen/PR spouse/common-law partner/parent employed with CAF, federal public admin, or provincial/territorial public service
  • C. Minor child (under 18) who is PR applying with a PR parent (child does NOT need to meet residency)
Fast-Track — Canadian Armed Forces (2014/2015 Amendments)
Expedited access to citizenship for persons serving or who have served in/with CAF.
✅ s.5(1.2) and s.5(1.3) exempt physical presence requirement of s.5(1)(c) if applicant served in/with CAF for 3 years (1,095 days) out of last 6-year period. All other requirements apply (language, knowledge, prohibitions).
✨

Special Grants — s.5(4) & s.5(5)

▼
s.5(4) — Minister's Discretionary Special Grant
Minister may grant citizenship at their discretion to any person to: (1) alleviate cases of statelessness, (2) alleviate special and unusual hardship, or (3) reward services of exceptional nature to Canada.

Rare and exceptional grant. Examples where it may be considered:

UN official whose primary residence/family in Canada but must work abroad in conflict zones Potential Olympic athlete who competes abroad and cannot meet residency
s.5(5) — Special Grants for Statelessness
Applicable to those born outside Canada on/after April 17, 2009. Requirements:
  • Born outside Canada on or after April 17, 2009
  • Birth parent was Canadian citizen at time of birth
  • Less than 23 years of age at time of application
  • Meet residence requirement
  • Have always been stateless
  • NOT convicted of terrorism, treason, national security offences, war crimes, etc.

Waivers — Language, Knowledge, Oath, Prohibitions, Financial

▼
Language/Knowledge Waivers — s.5(3)(a)
Can be granted for language OR knowledge requirement due to: (1) medical condition (medical waiver) OR (2) compassionate grounds to alleviate special/unusual hardship (compassionate waiver).
💡 Medical Waiver Documents: Completed Request for Medical Opinion form [CIT 0547], medical opinion by physician, all supporting medical records/reports/evidence.
Officer Considerations for Waivers
  • Age of applicant
  • Personal situation of applicant and family
  • Presence of other non-medical hardship factors
  • Past living conditions (how did person become PR? Protected person?)
  • Length of time applicant has been in Canada
Oath Waiver — s.5(3)(c)
Requirement to take Oath may be waived on compassionate grounds if applicant prevented from understanding significance by reason of mental disability. Must provide: (1) medical opinion form + documentation of mental disability, (2) evidence of identity of legal guardian applying on behalf of applicant.
Minors Waivers — s.5(3)(b)
Person with custody to submit application on behalf of minor Residency (ONLY for minors — NO discretion for adults) Oath requirement (minors 14–17) due to mental disability
Prohibitions Waiver
Waiver on compassionate grounds for prohibitions where applicant currently charged/on trial/subject to appeal for indictable offence outside Canada. Examples: unfounded charge, unlikely to ever receive trial due to foreign judicial process. s.22(5) allows Minister to waive s.22(4) permanent prohibitions for "acts against Canada's national interests" (terrorism, treason, etc.).
Financial Waiver (2019)
✅ Mayes and JFCY v. Minister (consent order Jan 2019): Minister may waive citizenship processing fees to alleviate statelessness, special/unusual hardship, or reward exceptional services to Canada.
🪶

Indigenous Applicants — Special Protocols

▼
Unique protocols for persons registered as Indian under Indian Act seeking Canadian citizenship.
Key Points
  • Registration under Indian Act requires certain number of grandparents registered
  • If member of First Nation → right to reside on reserve lands
  • Entitlement to enter/remain in Canada confirmed by IRPA s.19(1)
  • NO requirement to apply for PR — time in Canada while registered = time as PR
  • Residency period deemed to commence: (a) upon entering Canada (if already registered) OR (b) date became registered (if not registered when entered)
  • Any day lawfully in Canada prior to registration counts as ½ day toward residency (if within assessment period)
✅ Process is same as for all PRs, except residency calculation accommodates time registered under Indian Act.

11 Prohibitions Under the Citizenship Act

▼
Three Main Categories
Criminality in Canada Foreign Criminality Misrepresentation
Complete List of 11 Prohibitions
  • (1) Being in jail, on probation, or parole in Canada
  • (2) Serving a sentence outside Canada
  • (3) Charged/on trial for/subject of appeal for indictable offence in or outside Canada
  • (4) In 4 years before application, convicted of indictable offence OR offence under Citizenship Act in Canada
  • (5) In 4 years before application, convicted of indictable offence outside Canada
  • (6) Being under a removal order
  • (7) Subject of war crimes/crimes against humanity investigation or charge in Canada
  • (8) Convicted of war crimes in Canada
  • (9) Had citizenship refused for misrepresentation in past 5 years
  • (10) Had citizenship revoked for false representation, fraud, or knowingly concealing material circumstances in past 10 years
  • (11) As PR, convicted of terrorism, high treason, treason, spying offences OR served as member of armed forces of country engaged in armed conflict against Canada
⚖️

Criminality in Canada — s.22(2)

▼
Conviction for indictable offence under Act of Parliament leads to 4-year prohibition from applying for or being granted citizenship.
Two Key Distinctions from IRPA
A. When Prohibition Runs
  • Citizenship Act: From date of conviction
  • IRPA: From completion of sentence
B. Hybrid Offences
  • Citizenship Act: No deeming provision — Crown's election is dispositive
  • IRPA: Hybrid = automatically indictable
Ahmed, 2009 FC 672
Election of Crown prosecutor (whether to pursue as indictable or summary) is dispositive under Citizenship Act.
Time NOT Counted Toward Residency — s.21(a)–(c)
Cannot count any time spent under probation, on parole, or serving term of imprisonment.
Exception: Time on probation for conditional discharge Exception: If pardon/record suspension granted Exception: If convicted under Youth Criminal Justice Act (or previous Young Offenders Act)
🌍

Foreign Criminality

▼
Foreign Charge — s.22(1)(b.1)
Prohibited from citizenship while ongoing charge, trial, or appeal for foreign offence that could be equivalent of indictable offence under Act of Parliament.
✅ Discretionary waiver by Minister is possible (e.g., unfounded charge abroad, unlikely to receive trial due to foreign judicial process).
Foreign Conviction — s.22(3)
Conviction for foreign offence that could be equivalent of indictable offence under Act of Parliament leads to 4-year prohibition to being granted citizenship. Also prohibited while serving sentence abroad for such offence (s.19(1)(a.1)).
  • Recall equivalency analysis under IRPA s.36(1)(b), (2)(b) [Hill approach]
  • Unlike domestic criminality, does NOT matter how prosecutor treated charge abroad or how it may have been treated in Canada
⚠️ No discretion or H&C waiver possible for foreign conviction prohibition.
📄

Misrepresentation — s.22(1)(e.1)(e.2)

▼
Prohibited from citizenship for 5 years if person directly or indirectly misrepresents or withholds material circumstances relating to relevant matter, which induces or could induce an error in administration of Act.
Key Principles (Contrast with Revocation)
  • No mens rea or intent required (unlike revocation cases)
  • Sufficient if could induce an error (doesn't need to actually induce error)
  • Indirect misrepresentation counts
  • Withholding or omitting material circumstances counts
How This Differs from IRPA
ProvisionDurationMens Rea Required?
Citizenship Act s.22 (prohibition)5 yearsNO
IRPA s.40 (inadmissibility)5 yearsNO
Citizenship Act s.10 (revocation)10 yearsYES
🔍

Investigations — s.23.1

▼
Minister may require applicant to provide any additional information or evidence relevant to their application and require applicant to appear in person or by telecommunication to be examined before Minister (citizenship official) or citizenship judge.
⚠️ Counsel should caution clients of consequences — statements made can be used against them.
⏸️

Suspensions — s.13.1

▼
Minister may suspend processing of application for as long as necessary to receive any information or evidence or results of any investigation/inquiry for purpose of ascertaining whether applicant meets requirements or should be subject to admissibility hearing or removal order under IRPA.
💡 Significant latitude for Minister to suspend applications pending investigations. No fixed timeline.
📭

Abandonment

▼
Application can be treated as abandoned when applicant does NOT comply with s.23.1 by failing to provide requested documentation/information or to attend event when requested.
Abandonment Process
  • 30 days to provide response
  • 2 notices may be sent with additional 30 days provided
  • Reasonable explanation for inability to comply is required
Controversial Policy — Zhao (2016 FC 207), Kamel (2017 FC 946)
Cases litigated Minister's authority to declare abandonment and what constitutes "reasonable explanation."
⚖️

Federal Court Options

▼
Judicial Review
Available if application is refused, declared abandoned, or negative decision made (e.g., denying medical/compassionate waiver). Must apply within 30 days.
Mandamus Application
Request to Federal Court to compel Minister to perform a duty owed. See Gentile v. MCI, 2020 FC 452.
⚠️ RCIC Role: RCICs can assist in preparing applications but should refer clients to lawyer for Federal Court litigation.

📊

Revocation — Burden of Proof & Elements

▼
Burden of Proof
Minister's burden to establish required elements on balance of probabilities (Canada (MCI) v. Halindintwali, 2015 FC 390 at para.32).
💡 Balance of probabilities standard satisfied if evidence establishes it is more probable than not that something occurred (Rogan v. Canada (MCI), 2011 FC 1007 at paras.27–29).
Materiality
Misrepresentation of material fact includes: (1) an untruth, (2) withholding of truthful information, or (3) misleading answer which has effect of foreclosing or averting further inquiries (Canada v. Brooks, 1973 CanLII 5 (SCC) at 873).

Includes material omissions where there is duty of candour and providing intentionally misleading answers.

Material character of concealed facts depends on "significance for purposes of decision in question of information not disclosed" (Odynsky, 2001 FCT 138 at para.156).
Mens Rea (Intent) — KEY DIFFERENCE from Prohibition
⚠️ Minister must establish individual acted intentionally by deliberately concealing material circumstances or making false representations with intent to mislead decision maker (Canada (MCI) v. Savic, 2014 FC 523 at paras.66–76).
💡 This mens rea requirement differs from misrepresentation under s.22 of Citizenship Act or s.40 of IRPA, which do NOT require intent to mislead and encompass even unintentional, inadvertent, or indirect misrepresentations.
⚖️

Revocation — Procedural Rights (Hassouna, 2017 FC 473)

▼
According to Hassouna, procedurally fair revocation process requires:
Core Procedural Rights
  • Oral hearing before Court OR fair opportunity before independent tribunal
  • Fair opportunity to state case and know case to be met
Additional Requirements
  • Right to impartial and independent decision-maker
  • Consideration of personal interests or H&C factors

Changing Legal Landscape — Children & Revocation

▼
⚠️ Under s.10 of Citizenship Act and Zakaria, 2014 FC 864: A child may be vulnerable to revocation of citizenship for misrepresentations or omissions made by their parents many years in the past when the family obtained citizenship.

This is true regardless of:

  • How many years ago it occurred
  • Whether the child had any personal culpability
  • Whether the child had intention to mislead
💡 This area of law is evolving and may be subject to Charter challenges in the future.

Renunciation of Citizenship — s.9

▼
A person may give up citizenship if they are:
  • (a) Citizen of another country
  • (b) NOT subject of declaration by Governor in Council under s.20 (security/organized criminality)
  • (c) NOT a minor
  • (d) NOT having mental disability that prevents understanding significance of renunciation
  • (e) Does NOT reside in Canada
✅ Waiver on H&C grounds for (d) and (e) is possible.
↩️

Resumption of Citizenship — s.11

▼
Person may resume citizenship if they meet ALL requirements:
  • Submit an application
  • Were NOT subject of order under s.10 (citizenship was not revoked)
  • NOT subject to declaration under s.20 (security declaration)
  • NOT prohibited
  • NOT under removal order
  • Have become permanent resident after ceasing to be Canadian citizen (no unfulfilled conditions)
  • Physically present in Canada as PR for at least 365 days during 2 years immediately before application
  • Met applicable Income Tax Act requirement to file return for taxation year immediately before application year
