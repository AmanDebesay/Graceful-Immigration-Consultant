IRPA Objectives — Why Economic Immigration?

▼
IRPA s.3(1) — Objectives of the Act (Selected)
  • s.3(1)(a) — Permit Canada to pursue maximum social, cultural and economic benefits of immigration
  • s.3(1)(c) — Support development of a strong and prosperous Canadian economy, with benefits shared across all regions
  • s.3(1)(e) — Promote successful integration of PRs into Canada (mutual obligations)
  • s.3(1)(j) — Work with provinces to recognize foreign credentials and support rapid integration
IRPA s.3(3) — How the Act Must Be Applied
Key Principles
  • Furthers domestic and international interests of Canada
  • Promotes accountability and transparency
  • Consistent with the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms
  • Complies with international human rights instruments
IRPA s.12(2) — Economic Class
  • A foreign national may be selected as a member of the economic class on the basis of their ability to become economically established in Canada

National Occupational Classification (NOC 2021)

▼
NOC 2021 replaced NOC 2016. The key change: TEER (Training, Education, Experience, and Responsibilities) categories replace old Skill Levels 0, A, B, C, D.
TEER Categories — Second digit of the NOC code
TEERDescriptionEligible for Economic PR?
0Management occupationsYES
1University degree typically requiredYES
2College diploma or apprenticeship (2+ yrs)YES
3College diploma or apprenticeship (<2 yrs)YES
4High school diploma or on-job trainingNO (generally)
5Short work demonstration or no formal educationNO (generally)
IRPR R75 — Skilled Worker Definition (NOC)
Under R75(2), a foreign national is a skilled worker if within the last 10 years they have: (a) accumulated 1 year full-time work in a TEER 0/1/2/3 occupation; (b) performed actions described in the lead statement; (c) performed a substantial number of main duties, including all essential duties.
Key Cases on NOC
Potla v. Canada — Lead Statements
Officer can refuse if applicant doesn't meet the NOC lead statement. No duty to alert applicant to deficiencies before refusing (no procedural fairness obligation for sufficiency issues).
Kumar v. Canada — "Substantial" Main Duties
Officer must assess the "pith and substance" of work performed. Whether applicant performed a "substantial number of main duties" is a discretionary judgment call — court gives deference. Onus is on applicant to present sufficient evidence.

Employment Reference Letters — Pitfalls & Ethics

▼
⚠️ Employment reference letters are among the most problematic documents in economic immigration applications.
Why They're Risky
  • Must confirm all NOC lead statement duties
  • Must confirm substantial main duties
  • Officers compare letter to NOC description precisely
  • Potla: No duty to notify of deficiency before refusing
Ethical Issues to Navigate
  • Client may ask you to help "improve" a letter
  • Letters from foreign employers hard to verify
  • Risk of fraud / misrepresentation (IRPA s.40)
  • Consultant must not assist in misrepresentation
✅ Best Practices: Review letter against NOC before submission; ensure all essential duties are specifically mentioned; flag deficiencies to client early; never alter or fabricate documents.
🏥

Medical Exams, Police Certs & Biometrics

▼
Medical Inadmissibility — IRPA s.38

A foreign national is inadmissible on health grounds if their health condition:

(a) Likely danger to public health (b) Likely danger to public safety (c) Might cause excessive demand on health/social services
R70(1) + IRPA ss.20(1)(a) and 21(1): To obtain a PR visa, applicant must NOT be inadmissible. Family members (accompanying or not) must also not be inadmissible — IRPA s.42.
Biometrics — IRPA s.10.01 / IRPR s.12.1–12.91
Biometrics = fingerprints + photograph + biographic data. Required for ages 14–79 (R12.2(1)(a) and (b)). Purpose: ensure person granted PR visa is same person who arrives in Canada. Connects to misrepresentation under IRPA s.40(1)(a).

Qualifying Arranged Employment — MI EE s.29(2)

▼
Two Types of Arranged Employment & Points
LMIA-Based (200 pts)
  • Valid LMIA from ESDC
  • Offer must be full-time, continuous, non-seasonal
  • NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3
  • At least 1 year employment
Non-LMIA (50 pts)
  • Work permit under R204 (international agreements)
  • Work permit under R205 (Canadian interests)
  • Must have worked with employer for 1 year
  • Offer must meet same conditions
⚠️ Agbai v. Canada (2019 FC 886): Applicant lost 200 arranged employment points because LMIA expired before e-APR submission. Score dropped below lowest-ranked person in her ITA round — application refused.
⚖️

IRPA s.11.2 — The Post-ITA Assessment

▼
Officers assess whether the applicant STILL meets EE requirements at time of ITA AND at time of e-APR submission.
Two-Part Test (s.11.2)
  • Did applicant possess the qualifications declared in their e-APR, corroborated by supporting documentation?
  • Has information changed materially since the ITA such that they would NOT have been issued an ITA?
Outcomes
SituationResult
CRS at e-APR ≥ lowest-ranked score in ITA roundMay proceed if documentation corroborates claims
CRS at e-APR < lowest-ranked score in ITA roundREFUSED — did not maintain qualifying score
Birthday between ITA and e-APR causes score dropException — will not cause refusal
Singh v. Canada, 2017 FC 266
No duty to assist applicant or advise of concerns arising from applicant's failure to submit sufficient evidence. Officer was not satisfied that applicant had a qualifying offer of employment — no obligation to notify.

CEC — Employee vs Self-Employed

▼
Officers must determine whether CEC work experience was as an employee (qualifies) or self-employed (does NOT qualify). Key factors:
Points Toward Employee
  • Employer controls how/when work is done
  • Tools/equipment provided by employer
  • Work integrated into employer's business
  • Fixed hours and location
  • Payroll deductions (CPP, EI, taxes)
Points Toward Self-Employed
  • Provides own tools
  • Hires own helpers
  • Can profit or suffer losses
  • Works for multiple clients
  • Sets own hours/location
💰

FSW — Settlement Funds & Substituted Evaluation

▼
Settlement Funds
  • Must be applicant's own readily available funds (cannot be borrowed)
  • Exempt IF working in Canada AND have a valid job offer
  • "Valid" = LMIA-based WP, or employer-based WP with 1+ year of experience
  • Job must be TEER 0/1/2/3 — cannot just be LMIA alone, must be working too
Substituted Evaluation
⚠️ Substituted evaluation is limited in scope:
  • Can ONLY overcome the points assessment (67-point grid)
  • CANNOT overcome failure to meet minimum eligibility criteria or settlement fund requirements
  • Fact that applicant "almost met" requirements is NOT sufficient grounds alone
  • It is NOT humanitarian and compassionate authority (A25(1))

Procedural Fairness Letters (PFLs)

▼
A PFL is issued when an officer has concerns that may lead to refusal — the officer must give the applicant a chance to respond before deciding. This is the "right to be heard."
When Is a PFL Required?
Type of ConcernPFL Required?
Sufficiency (duties don't match NOC)NO — no PFL needed (Potla)
Credibility (applicant not believable)YES — PFL required
Veracity (truthfulness/accuracy)YES — PFL required
Authenticity (document may be fake)YES — PFL required
Steps to Respond to a PFL
  • Request the GCMS notes (via ATIP) to understand officer's full concerns
  • Identify all specific concerns raised in the PFL
  • Research applicable regulations and operational instructions
  • Gather responsive documents and evidence for each concern
  • Draft a detailed letter addressing each concern with legal references
  • Submit response before the deadline
Kapoor Decision — Key Warning
Failure to respond effectively to a PFL may lead to refusal. Such refusals are difficult to challenge on judicial review due to the deference courts show immigration officers.
⚖️

Standard of Review — Reasonableness vs Correctness

▼
Reasonableness
  • More than one "reasonable" outcome possible
  • Court must accept any decision in the range of acceptable outcomes
  • Decision must show: justification, transparency, intelligibility
  • Standard for most administrative decisions (Dunsmuir)
Correctness
  • Only ONE right answer in light of law and facts
  • Court may substitute its own view
  • Applied to constitutional questions, jurisdictional issues
Procedural Fairness Requirements
  • Meaningful opportunity to take part in the process
  • Essence of decision-maker's concerns communicated to applicant
  • Applicant advised of specific provisions of the Act at issue
  • Reasonable opportunity to respond given

Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) — Overview

▼
Constitution s.95 gives provinces shared jurisdiction over immigration. PNP allows provinces to nominate foreign nationals for PR based on their own criteria.
Shared Jurisdiction — Who Does What
Province / Territory
  • Establishes own nomination criteria
  • Criteria must NOT be incompatible with IRPA/IRPR
  • Must align with federal-provincial bilateral agreements
  • Issues nomination certificate
Federal (IRCC)
  • Assesses eligibility for Provincial Nominee Class
  • Assesses admissibility under IRPA/IRPR
  • Makes final decision on PR application
  • Controls processing via IRPR 87.1–87.2
Two Streams
Base Nominations (Non-EE)
  • Province nominates applicant
  • FN applies directly to IRCC for PR
  • Slower processing — not prioritized
Enhanced Nominations (via EE)
  • PNP nomination = +600 CRS points
  • Virtually guarantees ITA
  • FN must still meet FSW/CEC/FST requirements
🍁

Quebec Skilled Workers — Special Framework

▼
Quebec has a unique, autonomous selection system under the Canada-Quebec Accord. IRCC does NOT use Express Entry for Quebec applicants.
IRPR R86(2) — Membership in Quebec Skilled Worker Class
A foreign national is a member of the Quebec skilled worker class if they: (a) intend to reside in the Province of Quebec; and (b) are named in a Certificat de sélection du Québec (CSQ) issued to them by that Province.
⚠️ 2025 Note: The Regular Skilled Worker Program is being replaced by the Skilled Worker Selection Program. Invitations to apply are currently suspended until June 2025 or until new guidelines are announced.
🌊

Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP)

▼
Established January 2022 under IRPR 87.3. Covers the four Atlantic provinces: Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, and Newfoundland and Labrador.
AIP Requirements
  • Provincial endorsement certificate and intention to reside in that province
  • One year work experience requirement OR international graduate pathway
  • Full-time job offer from a designated Atlantic employer
  • Meet language and education requirements
  • 3-step application process: employer offer → provincial endorsement → IRCC application
Exemption Code
C18 — Atlantic Immigration Program (IMP work permit for AIP applicants)

Start-Up Visa (SUV) Program

▼
4 Core Eligibility Requirements
  • Have a qualifying business
  • Get a letter of support from a designated organization
  • Meet language requirements
  • Bring sufficient settlement funds
Designated Organizations — IRPR R98.03
Type# DesignatedMin. Investment Threshold
Business Incubators42Acceptance into program (no min. $)
Angel Investor Groups8$75,000 minimum
Venture Capital Funds26$200,000 minimum
To be designated, an organization must belong to: the Canadian Venture Capital & Private Equity Association (VCs) or the National Angel Capital Organization (angel investors and business incubators).
Qualifying Business Requirements
  • Applicant must hold at least 10% of voting rights in the business
  • Designated organization + all applicants together must hold more than 50% of voting rights
  • Business must be incorporated in Canada
  • Must be an "essential person" — active and central role in the business

Self-Employed Persons Program

▼
⚠️ Currently PAUSED until January 2027. Know the requirements for exam purposes.
IRPR R88(1) — Definition of "Self-Employed Person"
A foreign national who has: (1) relevant experience; (2) intention and ability to be self-employed in Canada; (3) intention and ability to make a significant contribution to specified economic activities in Canada.
Relevant Experience Requirements
Cultural Activities
  • 2 one-year periods of self-employment in cultural activities, OR
  • 2 one-year periods at world-class level in cultural activities, OR
  • Combination of 1 year each
  • E.g., music teachers, painters, filmmakers, choreographers, set designers
Athletics
  • 2 one-year periods of self-employment in athletics, OR
  • 2 one-year periods at world-class level in athletics, OR
  • Combination of 1 year each
  • "World class" = known internationally OR performs at highest levels in discipline
💡 Minimum 2 years of relevant experience in the 5 years before the application date.
Key Data

2018: 757 admissions (Start-Up + Self-Employed combined) | 2022: 1,364 admissions
