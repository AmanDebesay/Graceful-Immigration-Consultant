IRPA Family Class Framework — Core Structure

▼
Who Can Sponsor (IRPR 130)
  • ≥18 years old
  • Canadian citizen, PR, or registered Indian
  • If citizen abroad: must show plan to live in Canada
  • Not on social assistance (except disability)
  • Can provide for basic needs
Who Can Be Sponsored (R117)
  • Spouse (married/common-law/conjugal)
  • Dependent child
  • Parents & grandparents
  • Orphaned sibling/niece/nephew/grandchild
  • Person <18 to adopt
  • One other relative (if no other sponsorable)
Sponsor Prohibitions (R133)
  • Sexual offence conviction
  • Violence (≥10 yrs max) against person
  • Bodily harm to family/relative/conjugal partner
  • Default on previous sponsorship
  • Under removal order
  • In detention

Who Can Sponsor & Family Class Definition

Sponsor eligibility (IRPR 130/133), Family Class (R117), conjugal relationships, M v H, R117(9) exclusions
✅

Sponsor Eligibility — IRPR s.130

▼
You can sponsor your spouse, partner, or dependent child if:
  • You're at least 18 years old
  • You're a Canadian citizen, permanent resident of Canada, OR person registered in Canada as an Indian under the Canadian Indian Act
  • If you're a Canadian citizen living outside Canada: must show you plan to live in Canada when sponsored persons become PRs (cannot sponsor if PR living outside Canada)
  • You're not receiving social assistance for reasons other than disability
  • You can provide for the basic needs of persons you want to sponsor
Sponsor Not Residing in Canada — s.130(2)
A sponsor who is a Canadian citizen and does NOT reside in Canada may sponsor a foreign national who is the sponsor's spouse, common-law partner, conjugal partner, or dependent child with no dependent children, if the sponsor will reside in Canada when the FN becomes PR.
Special Income Requirements
💡 Parents/Grandparents: Income requirement of LICO + 30%
🚫

Sponsor Prohibitions — IRPR s.133(1)

▼
A sponsorship application shall only be approved if, on the day application filed AND from that day until decision is made, there is evidence that sponsor:
  • (b) Intends to fulfill obligations in sponsorship undertaking
  • (c) Is NOT subject to removal order
  • (d) Is NOT detained in any penitentiary, jail, reformatory, or prison
  • (e) Has NOT been convicted under Criminal Code of:
Three Categories of Criminal Convictions That Prohibit Sponsorship
(i) Sexual Offences
  • Offence of sexual nature
  • Attempt or threat to commit such offence
  • Against ANY person
(i.1) Indictable Violence
  • Indictable offence involving violence
  • Punishable by max ≥10 years
  • Or attempt to commit
  • Against ANY person
(ii) Bodily Harm to Specific Persons
  • Offence resulting in bodily harm
  • Or attempt/threat to commit
  • Against specific listed persons (see below)
Specific Persons for Bodily Harm Prohibition — s.133(1)(e)(ii)
  • (A) Current or former family member of sponsor
  • (B) Relative of sponsor + current/former family member of that relative
  • (C) Relative of sponsor's family member + current/former family member of that relative
  • (D) Current or former conjugal partner of sponsor
  • (E) Current or former family member of sponsor's family member or conjugal partner
  • (F) Relative of sponsor's conjugal partner + current/former family member of that relative
  • (G) Child under current/former care and control of sponsor, family member, or conjugal partner
  • (H) Child under current/former care and control of sponsor's relative or family member of that relative
  • (I) Someone sponsor is dating or has dated (whether or not lived together) + family member of that person

Family Class Members — IRPR s.117(1)

▼
According to IRPR 117(1), who qualifies as a member of the Family Class?
CategoryDescription
(a) SpouseCommon-law partner or conjugal partner
(b) Dependent ChildSee detailed definition below
(c) ParentsSponsor's parents
(d) GrandparentsSponsor's grandparents
(f) Orphaned RelativesOrphaned sibling, niece, nephew, or grandchild
(g) AdoptionPerson under 18 whom sponsor intends to adopt in Canada
(h) One Other RelativeIf sponsor has NO other sponsorable relative
👶

Dependent Child — IRPR s.2 Definition

▼
Dependent child means a child who has one of the following relationships with parent AND is in one of the following situations of dependency:
(a) Relationship with Parent
(i) Biological child of parent (if not adopted by person other than spouse/CL partner of parent) (ii) Adopted child of parent
(b) Situation of Dependency
(i) Under 22 Years Old
  • Less than 22 years of age
  • AND is NOT spouse or common-law partner
(ii) 22+ Years Old (Exception)
  • 22 years or older
  • Depended substantially on financial support of parent since before age 22
  • Unable to be financially self-supporting due to physical or mental condition
💍

Spouse: Married, Common-Law, Conjugal

▼
Married
  • In respect of marriage that took place outside Canada:
  • Marriage that is valid both under laws of jurisdiction where it took place AND under Canadian law
Common-Law Partner
  • Continuous cohabitation for at least one year
Conjugal Partner (IRPR s.2)
  • Foreign national residing outside Canada
  • In conjugal relationship with sponsor
  • Relationship for at least one year
What Does "Conjugal" Mean?
NOT defined in legislation. Determined by factors from M v H decision.
💡 IRCC Assessment: Conjugal partner category used when neither common-law partner status NOR marriage is possible (usually because of marital status or sexual orientation, combined with immigration barrier).

M v H — Seven Factors for Assessing Conjugal Relationships

▼
M v H (Supreme Court of Canada)
Established 7 factors that must be considered in assessing whether a conjugal relationship exists. These factors are NOT a checklist — all may not apply, and no single factor is determinative.
1. Shared Shelter

Do the parties live together? What are the living arrangements?

2. Sexual & Personal Behaviour

Intimate relationship, fidelity, commitment to each other

3. Services

Conduct and habits relating to preparation of meals, household tasks, mutual care

4. Social Activities

Joint social life, participation in each other's social circles, shared interests

5. Economic Support

Financial arrangements, ownership of property, sharing of household expenses

6. Children

Attitude and conduct toward children, parenting responsibilities

7. Social Perception

How the couple presents themselves publicly, reputation in the community

Critical Reflections on M v H
⚠️ Modern Relationship Challenges: The 7 factors were developed in context of traditional, cohabiting relationships. How well do they apply to: long-distance relationships, culturally diverse partnerships, non-traditional family structures, relationships affected by immigration barriers? Consider which factors you would add/remove for modern assessment.

Complex Issues & Inadmissibility

Misrepresentation — IRPA s.40

▼
When is a PR or FN inadmissible for misrepresentation under s.40 IRPA?
  • For directly or indirectly misrepresenting or withholding material facts relating to relevant matter that induces or could induce error in administration of Act
  • For being, or having been, sponsored by person who is inadmissible for misrepresentation
  • For having decision on claim/application for refugee protection vacated
  • For ceasing to be a citizen (under Citizenship Act)
Impact on Sponsorship
⚠️ Five-year ban (see s.40 IRPA). Cannot be sponsored to Canada during this period.
⚖️

Criminal Inadmissibility — IRPA s.36(2) for FNs

▼
In-Canada Offences
Convicted of indictable offence Convicted of two offences not arising from single occurrence
Foreign Offences
Convicted of (or committing) offence that, if in Canada, would be indictable Convicted of two offences not arising from single occurrence
On Entering Canada
Committing offence under Act of Parliament prescribed by regulations
Sponsor Prohibitions — s.133 IRPR
⚠️ s.133 IRPR prohibits sponsorship under certain conditions where sponsor has criminal convictions (see Module 1 for details on sexual offences, violence, bodily harm to family members).
Impact on Sponsorship
Permanent bar unless cured by way of criminal rehabilitation or record suspension.
🏥

Health Inadmissibility — IRPA s.38

▼
When is FN inadmissible on health grounds under s.38 IRPA?
Danger to Public Health
  • Health condition likely danger to public health
Danger to Public Safety
  • Health condition likely danger to public safety
Excessive Demand
  • Might reasonably be expected to cause excessive demand on health or social services
Exemptions from s.38(1) — Who is Exempt?
  • Member of Family Class + their dependent children
  • Applicant for PR visa as protected person
  • Protected person
  • Spouse, common-law partner, child, or family member of above categories
💰

Financial Inadmissibility — IRPA s.39

▼
When is FN inadmissible for financial reasons under s.39 IRPA?
  • Are, or will be, unable or unwilling to support themselves or any dependent person
  • Have NOT satisfied officer that adequate arrangements for care and support (other than social assistance) have been made

Building a Consultation Checklist

▼
When consulting with potential sponsors and applicants, systematically gather information to identify issues early.
Sample Consultation Questions
  • 1. Is the sponsor a Canadian citizen or permanent resident?
  • 2. If citizen: do they reside in Canada? If not, will they return when applicant becomes PR?
  • 3. Is the sponsor ≥18 years old?
  • 4. Is the sponsor on social assistance (except for disability)?
  • 5. Does the sponsor have any criminal convictions? (Sexual offences? Violence? Bodily harm to family members?)
  • 6. Is the sponsor subject to a removal order?
  • 7. Is the sponsor in detention?
  • 8. Has the sponsor defaulted on previous sponsorship undertakings?
  • 9. What is the relationship between sponsor and applicant? (Spouse/CL/conjugal/child/parent/etc.)
  • 10. How long has the relationship existed?
  • 11. If married: was marriage valid in jurisdiction where it took place? Valid under Canadian law?
  • 12. If common-law: have they cohabited continuously for ≥1 year?
  • 13. If conjugal: why can't they marry or cohabit? Immigration barrier? Legal barrier?
  • 14. Does the applicant have any inadmissibility issues? (Criminal? Medical? Misrepresentation? Financial?)
  • 15. Has the applicant ever been refused a Canadian visa or found inadmissible?
  • 16. What evidence exists to prove genuineness of relationship?

Evidence & Sponsorship Applications

In-Canada vs overseas, R4 bad faith, genuineness test, primary purpose test, evidence assessment
🇨🇦

In-Canada vs Overseas Applications

▼
In-Canada Application
  • Sponsored spouse must remain in Canada until decision
  • Cannot exit Canada or application may be abandoned
  • May be eligible for work permit (pilot project)
  • NO right of appeal to IAD
  • Difficulty overcoming inadmissibility
Overseas Application
  • Right of appeal to IAD
  • Often faster processing times
  • Only option for conjugal partners
  • Applicant can travel freely
  • Forms differ slightly — check IRCC website
🚫

Bad Faith Relationships — R4(1) IRPR

▼
How does s.4(1) IRPR define "bad faith" relationships?

A foreign national is not a member of the family class if the marriage, common-law partnership, or conjugal partnership:

  • Was entered into primarily for the purpose of acquiring status or privilege under the Act (primary purpose test)
  • Is not genuine (genuineness test)
Relevant Time-Frames — Zhou v Canada (2020 FC 633, para 44)
Primary Purpose Test
  • Assessed at time relationship entered into
Genuineness Test
  • Assessed ongoing — at time of application and decision
📊

Evidence to Assess Genuineness

▼
Acceptable Evidence for Genuineness
Birth certificates for children Statutory declaration of common-law union Proof of cohabitation for 1-year period (common-law partners) Proof of financial transfers, visits, communication Joint bank accounts, shared assets Photos together with family/friends over time Travel itineraries and boarding passes Phone records, chat logs, emails
Officer Subjectivity — Van v Canada (2009 CanLII 93234, paras 33-34)
⚠️ Potential for negative outcomes: Officers' personal biases and cultural assumptions can influence assessment of relationship evidence. Be aware of how evidence may be perceived and provide context where cultural practices differ from Canadian norms.
Birth of Child — Gill v Canada (2010 FC 122, para 8)
✅ Birth of child is relevant evidence of genuineness but is not determinative on its own. Officer must still assess all factors.
⚖️

Negative Inferences from Previous History

▼
Can Negative Inferences Be Drawn?
Thach v Canada (2008 FC 658, para 26)
YES — It was open to IAD to examine the immigration history of parties in assessing credibility and as one of relevant factors in assessing bona fides of marriage.
Zhou v Canada (2020 FC 633, para 61)
NOT AUTOMATIC — "I fail to see how it follows from the fact that Ms. Zhou married her second husband to secure PR for herself that she therefore married her third husband to secure PR for him."
💡 Key Takeaway: Previous immigration or marital history can be considered BUT cannot be the sole basis for negative inference. Each relationship must be assessed on its own merits.

Completing Sponsorship Forms & Client Consultation

▼
Mock Sponsorship Application — Discussion Points
  • What areas were difficult to complete?
  • What questions were missing from application forms that should have been asked?
  • What questions were confusing or unclear?
Consultant-Client Roleplay Activity
Practice Objective: Ask questions that would assist in completing government application forms. Focus on gathering relevant information efficiently and accurately.
How to Avoid Refusal Under R4
  • Provide comprehensive evidence of genuineness (photos, communication, visits over time)
  • Address relationship development timeline clearly
  • Explain any red flags proactively (age difference, short courtship, cultural practices)
  • Demonstrate commitment beyond immigration benefit
  • Include credible third-party evidence (family, friends who know relationship)
  • Be consistent across all forms and supporting documents

Burden & Standard of Proof

Magonza framework: credibility, probative value, weight, sufficiency, de novo hearings
⚖️

Evidence Admissibility — IRPA s.175(1) & s.182(1)

▼
Rules of Evidence — s.182(1)
The IAD:
  • (a) Must hold a hearing (appeals under s.63(4))
  • (b) Is NOT bound by any legal or technical rules of evidence
  • (c) Use evidence that it considers credible or trustworthy
💡 IAD hearings are de novo — fresh hearing on merits, not review of officer's decision. New evidence can be introduced.
📊

Magonza v Canada (2019 FC 14) — Four Key Concepts

▼
Magonza v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2019 FC 14
Context: Challenges of fact-finding in refugee law. Statements by relatives/friends take place outside Canada. Hard to deploy common fact-finding tools. Context of uncertainty leads to credibility questions.
Balancing Two IRPA Goals
Goal 1
  • Fair consideration to those who come claiming persecution
Goal 2
  • Fair and efficient procedures that maintain integrity of system
Four Concepts to Balance These Goals
ConceptDefinitionQuestion Asked
CredibilityTrustworthiness and reliability of evidence"Is this a trustworthy source of information?" / "Is the witness honest and telling the truth?" / "Does testimony provide accurate account of material facts?"
Probative ValueCapacity of evidence to establish the fact it is offered to prove"To what degree is this information useful in answering the question I have to address?"
WeightPersuasiveness of evidence in comparison with other evidence"What counts in the ultimate balancing?" Weight = Credibility × Probative Value
SufficiencyWhether evidence has enough weight to meet standard of proof"Am I convinced by the evidence that a fact is proven?"
🎯

Credibility Assessment Factors

▼
According to Magonza, what factors are used to assess credibility?
Demeanor of Witness
  • Body language, eye contact, tone, hesitation, confidence
Plausibility
  • Does the account make sense? Is it consistent with known facts?
Bias, Interest & Motivation
  • Does witness have reason to be untruthful? Stake in outcome?
⚖️

Burden of Proof — Who Bears It?

▼
Burden Rests with APPELLANT
  • Sponsorship appeals under s.63(1)
  • Residency obligation appeals under s.63(4)
  • Assessments of H&C under s.67(1)
Burden Rests with MINISTER
  • In a removal order appeal where:
  • Removal order made by ID under s.45(d)
  • Subject of s.44 report is a PR
⚠️ It is NOT up to Hearings Officer or Panel to identify issues or ask required questions (Vo v. MCI, 2014 FC 816). Burden is on the party who must prove the case.
📐

Standard of Proof

▼
"In the IAD, the panel must determine whether the necessary elements of the issues in the appellant's case have been established by the facts as found. The standard of proof varies according to the legal issue before the panel. As in the ID, some provisions of IRPA specify the applicable standard of proof."— IRB, "Weighing Evidence - Chapter 4: Standard of proof and burden of proof"
💡 Generally balance of probabilities standard applies in sponsorship appeals — more likely than not that the fact exists.
📖

Legal Principles

▼
A legal principle is any statement which is used, along with facts, to reach a conclusion.
Examples of Legal Principles
  • Laches (delay in asserting rights)
  • Good faith
  • Res judicata (matter already judged)
  • Impartiality of judges
  • Natural justice / procedural fairness

In immigration law: Principles like "family reunification," "best interests of the child," "genuineness of relationship" guide decision-making.

IAD & Federal Court Appeals

Right of appeal (s.62/63), IAD jurisdiction, H&C (s.65), Federal Court JR, ADR process
⚖️

IAD Right of Appeal — IRPA s.62 & s.63

▼
s.62 — IAD Jurisdiction
IAD is the competent Division of the Board with respect to appeals under this Division.
s.63(1) — Right of Appeal
A person who has filed (in prescribed manner) an application to sponsor a FN as member of Family Class may appeal to IAD against decision not to issue the FN a permanent resident visa.
🚫

No Right of Appeal — IRPA s.64(1)

▼
No right to appeal if FN or PR has been found to be inadmissible on grounds of:
Security Violating human or international rights Serious criminality Organized criminality
💚

H&C Considerations — IRPA s.65

▼
s.65 — In an appeal under s.63(1) or (2), the IAD may NOT consider H&C considerations unless it has decided:
  • FN is a member of the Family Class
  • Sponsor is a sponsor within the meaning of the regulations
💡 Two-Stage Analysis: IAD must first determine validity of refusal. Only if FN qualifies as Family Class member and sponsor is eligible can IAD proceed to H&C assessment to potentially allow appeal despite valid refusal.
📋

Filing an IAD Appeal

▼
Notice of Appeal — s.13 IAD Rules, 2022
To file appeal of decision, person must provide to Division a notice of appeal together with:
  • (a) In case of sponsorship appeal: officer's decision AND written reasons (if any) for refusal
Time Limit — s.16 IAD Rules, 2022
Unless notice provided to ID member under subrule 14(1), notice of appeal and accompanying documents must be received by Division no later than:
  • (a) In case of sponsorship appeal: 30 days after day on which appellant receives officer's decision and written reasons (if any) for refusal
⚖️

Federal Court — Right of Judicial Review

▼
Application for JR — IRPA s.72
s.72(1) — Judicial review by FC subject to s.86.1, commenced by making of application for leave to Court.
s.72(2)(a) — Application for JR may NOT be made until right of appeal is exhausted.
Jurisdiction — Federal Courts Act s.18.1
s.18.1(1) — Application for JR may be made by Attorney General of Canada OR by anyone directly affected by the matter.
s.18.1(2) — Application for JR shall be made:
  • Within 30 days after time decision/order was first communicated to party directly affected
  • Within any further time that FC judge may fix or allow before or after end of those 30 days
🔨

Federal Court Powers — s.18.1(3) Federal Courts Act

▼
On application for judicial review, FC may:
  • (a) Order federal board/commission/tribunal to do any act or thing it has unlawfully failed/refused to do or unreasonably delayed in doing
  • (b) Declare invalid or unlawful, or quash, set aside, or set aside and refer back for determination (with directions), prohibit or restrain, a decision/order/act/proceeding of federal board/commission/tribunal

ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) Process

▼
Getting to ADR
  • Can be selected by IAD on their own initiative
  • Counsel can request ADR
  • Lower threshold than full hearing — Jugpall v Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 1999 CanLII 20685 (CA IRB)
Benefits and Drawbacks
Pros
  • Faster resolution
  • Less adversarial
  • Cost-effective
  • Parties have more control over outcome
  • Can preserve relationships
Cons
  • May feel pressured to settle
  • Power imbalances
  • No precedent set
  • Limited appeal rights if settled
  • May not address systemic issues

CBSA Hearings Officer — Minister's Counsel Role

▼
Whom Does the CBSA Hearings Officer Represent?
Minister of Citizenship & Immigration
  • Appeals pertaining to visa refusal of Family Class
  • Residency obligation appeals re decisions made abroad
Minister of Public Safety
  • Removal order appeals
  • Ministerial appeals against decision by IRB Immigration Division
Role of Hearings Officer as Minister's Counsel
  • Ensure integrity of the system is upheld
  • Ensure justice is served
  • Defend decisions of officer not to issue visa
  • Defend decisions of Minister's delegate or ID to issue removal order
📊

Evidence & Burden in IAD Proceedings

▼
Can Hearings Officer Present New Evidence?
YES — Due to de novo nature of IAD hearings. New evidence often introduced through documentation at hearing.
Who Carries Burden of Proof?
Appellant (in sponsorship appeals under s.63(1)). It is NOT up to Hearings Officer or Panel to identify issues or ask required questions (Vo v. MCI, 2014 FC 816).
👤

Designated Representatives

▼
Who Decides Whether DR is Necessary?
IAD Member determines whether designated representative is necessary (e.g., for minors, persons with mental disability).
What Should Counsel Do?
If counsel knows of someone in Canada who meets requirements of DR, provide name and contact information to IAD [IAD Rule 46(a)].
⚖️

What Does the IAD Do?

▼
IAD examines cases for possible errors:
  • 1. Error in law
  • 2. Error in fact
  • 3. Error in mixed fact and law
  • 4. Failure to observe principle of natural justice
✅ IAD has authority to reverse valid decisions on H&C grounds — considers factors that may warrant appeal despite fact decision is valid in law.
🎤

Effective Advocacy — Best Practices

▼
Appearance & Body Language
  • Make a good first impression (professional attire)
  • Be aware of your mannerisms
  • Don't walk around hearing room too much
  • Maintain appropriate posture
Language
  • Use introductory sentences to take place of headings
  • Ensure language is appropriate — don't use "I believe," "I think," or "in my opinion"
  • Start with "I submit" or "It is the [party's] position that..." when expressing position/argument
Speaking & Delivery
  • Adjust volume to the room
  • Speak more slowly than you think necessary
  • Think of pacing as punctuation of spoken presentation — pause briefly between sentences, longer between ideas
  • Use voice expressively — do NOT speak in monotone
  • Raise and lower voice as appropriate
Additional Tips
  • Be attentive to decision-maker
  • Maintain direct eye contact with decision-maker while speaking
  • Don't memorize a prepared script — use outline instead
  • Practice your presentation
