5 Common Mistakes That Delay Your Immigration Application

Most processing delays aren't caused by complicated cases — they're caused by small, avoidable errors. Here are the five we see most often, and how to avoid them.

  1. Incomplete forms. A single blank mandatory field can trigger a return of your entire application. Double-check every section before submitting, including ones that feel repetitive.
  2. Inconsistent information across documents. Names, dates, and addresses should match exactly across your passport, forms, and supporting letters. Minor discrepancies raise questions that slow down review.
  3. Outdated supporting documents. Police certificates, medical exams, and language test results all have validity windows. Submitting an expired document means resubmitting later.
  4. Missing translations or certifications. Foreign-language documents generally need a certified translation attached. Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons for a request for additional information.
  5. Waiting too long to respond to requests. If an officer requests more information, there's usually a strict deadline. Missing it can result in refusal, regardless of how strong the rest of your application is.

Working with a regulated consultant helps catch these issues before submission, when they're easiest to fix.

Express Entry CRS Score: How to Improve Your Ranking

The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score determines whether you receive an invitation to apply for permanent residence through Express Entry. A few targeted improvements can meaningfully raise your score.

  • Improve your language test results. Language ability is one of the highest-weighted factors. Even a small jump in your CLB level across all four abilities can add significant points.
  • Add a provincial nomination. A provincial nomination adds 600 points — effectively guaranteeing an invitation. Research provincial programs that match your occupation and background.
  • Gain additional skilled work experience. Extra years of Canadian or foreign skilled work experience contribute to your score, especially when combined with strong language results.
  • Pursue further education. Completing an additional credential, particularly a Canadian one, can add points under the education factor.
  • Get a valid job offer. A qualifying arranged employment offer can add points and strengthen your overall profile.

A consultant can review your profile and identify which of these levers will move your score the most for your specific situation.

A Step-by-Step Guide to Spousal Sponsorship in Canada

Spousal sponsorship allows Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor their spouse or common-law partner for permanent residence. Here's what the process generally looks like.

  1. Confirm eligibility. The sponsor must be a Canadian citizen or PR, at least 18, and not receiving social assistance for reasons other than disability. The relationship must meet the legal definition of spouse or common-law partner.
  2. Gather your relationship evidence. Photos, communication records, joint finances, and statements from friends and family all help demonstrate a genuine relationship.
  3. Complete the sponsorship and application forms. The sponsor completes a sponsorship application while the applicant completes their permanent residence application — both are submitted together.
  4. Submit biometrics and complete a medical exam. Most applicants need to provide biometrics and pass an immigration medical exam.
  5. Wait for processing and respond to requests. Processing times vary, and IRCC may request additional documents or an interview before making a decision.

Because relationship evidence is scrutinized closely, having a consultant review your file before submission can reduce the chance of delays or refusal.

What Employers Need to Know About the LMIA Process

A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is often required before an employer can hire a foreign worker. Here's a plain-language overview of what's involved.

  • Confirm whether you need one. Some work permit categories are LMIA-exempt under trade agreements or specific programs — it's worth checking before starting the process.
  • Demonstrate recruitment efforts. Most LMIA streams require proof that you advertised the position domestically and could not find a qualified Canadian or permanent resident.
  • Prepare a compliant job offer. Wages, working conditions, and job duties must meet the applicable requirements for the occupation and region.
  • Submit the application with supporting documents. This includes business documents, recruitment evidence, and the employment contract.
  • Plan for processing time. LMIA processing times vary by stream and volume — building this into your hiring timeline avoids last-minute pressure.
